Pharmacy Operations & Launch Guide

Practical operating procedures, staff controls and evidence required before a real pharmacy launch.

Version 1.0 · 18 November 2026

This guide supports implementation. It is not a licence, legal opinion, clinical protocol, regulatory approval or proof that this software is production-ready.

STOP — do not use the demo data for real dispensing.
The browser prototype still stores ordinary stock, purchase, patient, claims and some report workflows in localStorage. Only specific API functions are database-backed. Never enter real patient/medical data or use fictional batch quantities. Launch only after the critical gates in this guide have been completed and independently tested.

1. What the system currently does

API / database implemented

  • Organization, branch and user registration with email verification.
  • Short-lived JWT login and password reset flow.
  • Branch-scoped access and role checks on API routes.
  • Product records and central medicine reference search.
  • Database purchase receiving endpoint with batch, expiry, supplier, price and movement records.
  • Database sales endpoint with stock checks and transactional deduction.
  • Batch quarantine and supplier-return/disposal events.
  • Routine stock inspection records and expiry reporting.
  • Audit events for selected operations.

Still demo / incomplete

  • Main dashboard metrics and ordinary inventory/purchase screens use browser-local sample data.
  • Patient directory and insurance claims are demo-only.
  • Some reports, transfers, barcode mappings and notifications are demo-only; API support exists for selected transfers/barcode lookup but UI integration is incomplete.
  • Role labels shown in the UI are not a substitute for server-enforced permission tests.
  • No configured production hosting, real backups, recovery drill, monitoring or regulator approval is included.

2. Recommended daily pharmacy workflow

  1. Opening check: confirm the responsible pharmacist is present and authorized; check premises cleanliness, security, refrigerator/storage temperatures where applicable, power and environmental logs.
  2. Receive delivery: compare supplier invoice and purchase order; verify supplier authorization, product identity, strength, dosage form, pack, Rwanda registration/authorization where applicable, intact packaging, batch/lot, expiry, quantity and transport/storage condition.
  3. Record batch: create or select the exact product; enter the supplier, invoice, lot number, expiry, received quantity, unit cost and selling price. Never invent missing lot or expiry data. Reject or quarantine discrepancies.
  4. Put away: store according to manufacturer label and approved procedures; segregate expired, damaged, recalled, returned, suspect, controlled and cold-chain items as applicable. Maintain FEFO (first expiry, first out).
  5. Dispense / sell: confirm patient/customer, product, strength, form, quantity, prescription/legal status and pharmacist review where required. Select an eligible batch, counsel appropriately, record the transaction and issue the required receipt.
  6. Close of day: reconcile cash/mobile-money/card totals against recorded sales; review voids/returns, stock discrepancies, low-stock and expiry alerts; secure controlled medicines and records; sign the closing checklist.

These are operating principles, not a substitute for Rwanda-specific approved SOPs or current treatment/dispensing guidance.

3. How the key features work

Stock by batch

A medicine product is the catalogue identity; a batch is a specific manufacturer lot with its own expiry, supplier, cost, selling price and quantity. Stock must be reconciled by batch, not just by medicine name. The barcode normally identifies a product pack, not necessarily the physical lot or expiry; staff must read and verify those from the pack.

FEFO and expiry control

FEFO means eligible stock with the earliest expiry is used first. Expired, quarantined, recalled, damaged or otherwise blocked stock must not be available for sale. Review near-expiry stock on a defined schedule and record returns or disposal with authorization and evidence.

Quarantine / recall

Immediately segregate the affected batch physically and electronically; block it from dispensing; record the reason, quantity, date, responsible staff and reference; identify affected transactions/customers where required; follow the supplier/Rwanda FDA recall instruction; document final disposition and pharmacist/manager sign-off.

Audit trail

Audit events provide accountability for selected API operations. Production acceptance must test every critical action, restrict audit access, define retention, monitor suspicious activity and ensure records cannot be silently altered by ordinary users.

Branch access

Each staff account should only access assigned branches and permitted functions. Use individual accounts; never share passwords. Remove access immediately when staff leave or change roles.

4. Staff roles and responsibility

  • Owner / administrator: business governance, account creation, branch access, supplier approval, finance and system configuration. Do not override clinical decisions.
  • Responsible licensed pharmacist: professional oversight, prescription review, clinical decisions, controlled/regulated medicine procedures, recalls, SOP approval, staff supervision and final clinical sign-off.
  • Stock controller: receiving checks, batch entry, storage, stock counts, expiry checks, quarantine and documented adjustments.
  • Cashier: authorized sales and payment recording only; no unrestricted admin or clinical privileges.
  • Claims/insurance officer: minimum necessary insurance information and claim follow-up under written authorization.

Role design in the interface is illustrative. Validate and enforce all permissions on the server before go-live.

5. Required launch evidence checklist

  • Rwanda FDA premise registration and operational licence applicable to the exact premises and activity.
  • National Pharmacy Council registration and current practising licence for responsible pharmacy professionals.
  • Approved premises, layout, storage, sanitation, security, equipment and temperature-monitoring evidence.
  • Approved supplier list, supplier authorization checks and verified product registration/market authorization checks.
  • Signed SOPs: procurement/receiving, storage, dispensing, prescription handling, controlled medicines, cold chain, returns, recalls, quarantine, waste, complaints, incidents, cleaning, temperature excursions, stock counts and business continuity.
  • Staff files, role descriptions, training records, competency checks and duty rosters.
  • Opening physical stock count reconciled to real product/batch/expiry/quantity/cost records; remove all fictional demo records.
  • Data protection assessment: controller/processor role, registration obligations, privacy notice, processing inventory, lawful basis, retention, access/rectification processes, processor agreements and breach response.
  • Production security: HTTPS, production secrets, restricted database access, MFA for privileged users, account lifecycle, monitoring and vulnerability review.
  • Encrypted automated backups, off-site copy, tested restore, recovery objectives and incident-response contact list.
  • Documented user acceptance test results, pharmacist sign-off, unresolved defect register and rollback plan.
  • Written confirmation from competent authorities/advisers on tax, invoicing, records, controlled drugs and insurer requirements that apply to the business.

6. Rwanda regulatory references to verify

  • Rwanda FDA: premise licensing, inspections and Good Distribution Practice expectations for wholesale/retail medical-product premises. Check current requirements directly with the Authority.
  • National Pharmacy Council: registration and annual practising licence for pharmacy professionals; consult current professional guidelines and Good Pharmacy Practice materials.
  • Data protection: Law Nº 058/2021 relating to protection of personal data and privacy applies to personal-data processing. Health and medical records are sensitive personal data; determine registration, privacy notice, security and other obligations with the Data Protection & Privacy Office or qualified counsel.
  • Product and controlled-medicine rules: verify the current product register, prescription classification, controlled-drug requirements, import/supply permissions and recall notices for each product/category.

Official references: Rwanda FDA · National Pharmacy Council · Data Protection & Privacy Office.

7. Technical launch sequence

  1. Choose and secure the production host and managed PostgreSQL service; keep database private.
  2. Configure unique production secrets (JWT secret at least 32 random characters), SMTP, exact HTTPS app origin, environment and trusted proxy settings. Never commit .env.
  3. Run schema/migrations against a new production database; create owner account using verified business email; verify email; assign branch and least-privilege roles.
  4. Disable public medicine availability until product and stock data have been validated and the pharmacist/owner authorizes publication.
  5. Connect all live screens to API/database; remove or hard-disable demo writes. Do not mix localStorage records with production records.
  6. Configure monitoring, logs, encrypted backups, retention, restore test, incident alerts and access reviews.
  7. Complete the acceptance test plan and resolve all critical/high defects before launch.
